A personal injury firm relies on quite a lot of software to run its business. There is the case management system (CMS), of course, but there are also the claims portals, medical reporting services, document systems, compliance checks and financial tools that sit alongside it.

Most firms will have a system such as Proclaim, Visualfiles, LEAP, Smokeball or Clio at the centre of their operation. This is where the case file lives, where fee earners manage their work and where the progress of a claim is recorded.

But here's the thing. Having a case management system doesn't necessarily mean that all the work around managing a case is automated.

A typical personal injury claim can pass through several different systems before it reaches settlement. Information has to be entered, documents have to be reviewed, insurers and medical agencies have to be chased, and important dates have to be monitored. Much of this work still involves someone moving information from one place to another.

So, what software does a UK personal injury firm actually need, and where are the opportunities to make things more efficient?

It's worth looking at the different parts of the technology stack, because the answer is rather more involved than simply choosing a case management system.

## The software used by a personal injury firm

Most claimant firms have a core case management system, supported by a collection of other services that handle specific parts of the claims process.

Although the exact combination varies between firms, the main components tend to look something like this:

| Software or service | What it does | Typical examples |
|---|---|---|
| Case management system | Holds case files, documents, tasks, correspondence and financial information | Proclaim, Visualfiles, LEAP, Smokeball, Clio, ActionStep, Aderant |
| Claims Portal | Manages low-value RTA, employers' liability and public liability claims | Claims Portal |
| Official Injury Claim | Handles qualifying whiplash claims on the small claims track | Official Injury Claim service |
| Medical reporting | Sources medical reports for eligible personal injury claims | MedCo and medical reporting organisations |
| Onboarding and compliance | Handles client identification, AML checks, client care and signatures | ID checking and e-signature services |
| Records and evidence | Obtains and processes medical records, engineering reports and other evidence | Records agencies and document review tools |
| Accounts and costs | Manages disbursements, invoices and costs drafting | CMS accounts modules and separate finance systems |

Each of these services has its own purpose, and in many cases firms have little choice about whether to use them. The Claims Portal, for example, is part of the prescribed claims process, while MedCo governs the sourcing of medical reports for relevant claims.

The problem is that these systems don't necessarily talk to one another particularly well. Even when integrations exist, there can still be a surprising amount of work left for people to do.

To understand why, it helps to start with the system at the centre of everything.

## Which case management systems do UK personal injury firms use?

The case management system is usually the most important software decision a firm makes. It holds the case file, manages tasks and deadlines, stores correspondence and generates much of the documentation needed to progress a claim.

It is also one of the hardest systems to replace. Firms often spend years configuring their CMS around the way they work, building templates, workflows and integrations that support their particular business.

There are several established systems in the UK market.

There are two important things to take away from this.

Firstly, these systems are designed to manage legal cases. They provide the structure, records and processes that a personal injury firm needs to operate. Choosing between them is largely a question of which system best suits the firm's size, case types, budget and preferred way of working.

Secondly, and perhaps more importantly, they differ in how far they extend beyond the case file itself.

How well does the system connect to the Claims Portal? What happens when a medical report arrives? Can information be extracted from a document and put straight into the relevant fields? What happens when an insurer responds or a deadline approaches?

These are the sorts of questions that matter when you're dealing with hundreds or thousands of live cases.

In fact, if you're evaluating a new CMS, I'd suggest asking each supplier to demonstrate an entire claim, from the first client contact through to the stage 2 settlement pack. Don't just look at the screens and the features. Watch what actually happens and count how many times someone still has to type information that already exists somewhere else.

There is also another consideration. Many established claimant firms have spent years configuring their existing systems. Replacing them can be a substantial undertaking, with considerable cost and disruption to the business.

Most firms we speak to aren't necessarily looking to replace their case management system. They want to get more out of the technology they already have.

## The Claims Portal, Official Injury Claim and MedCo

Alongside the CMS, personal injury firms have to use several specialist services that form part of the claims process.

These aren't optional pieces of business software. They exist because the claims process itself requires them.

**The Claims Portal**

The Claims Portal handles low-value road traffic accident (RTA), employers' liability (EL) and public liability (PL) claims up to £25,000 under the relevant pre-action protocols.

Claims progress through a series of stages, each with its own requirements and response deadlines. Miss a deadline or fail to complete a required action and the claim may no longer be eligible to continue through the portal.

Firms can use the portal through its own interface or access it through their case management system. The latter is generally achieved through an application-to-application (A2A) connection, which allows the CMS and portal to exchange information.

Software suppliers build these integrations under agreements with Claims Portal Ltd.

**The Official Injury Claim service**

The Official Injury Claim (OIC) service handles qualifying whiplash claims under £5,000 on the small claims track. It was introduced on 31 May 2021 and is operated by the Motor Insurers' Bureau (MIB) on behalf of the Ministry of Justice.

The service was designed to allow individuals to pursue eligible claims without legal representation, although represented claimants and their solicitors use it too.

It has its own process, stages, tariff and deadlines. And, as with the Claims Portal, information needs to be kept up to date in the firm's case management system.

Where a firm has to work directly through the OIC interface, this can mean entering the same information more than once. It is a relatively straightforward example of the sort of manual work that automation can remove.

Toca, for example, uses software bots to interact with the OIC screens in much the same way a fee earner would. Information can be transferred between the portal and the firm's existing case management system without someone having to rekey it.

**MedCo**

MedCo was introduced in April 2015 to provide a regulated system for sourcing medical reports in relevant road traffic accident claims.

Firms commissioning medical reports must register with MedCo and declare any relevant financial links. The service connects solicitors with medical reporting organisations and experts.

It is another important part of the claims process, but it is not a case management system and doesn't remove the need to keep the firm's own records updated.

And this brings us to the underlying issue.

None of these services replaces the case management system, and none automatically guarantees that everything recorded in one place is reflected in another. Every time a claim progresses, someone or something needs to make sure that the relevant dates, offers, documents and tasks are updated.

That is where a lot of the work starts to accumulate.

## Where personal injury firms lose time between systems

If you walk through the lifecycle of a typical personal injury claim, you'll find that a considerable amount of work happens outside the case management system itself.

The CMS might know that a medical report is required, for example, but that doesn't necessarily mean it can obtain the report, read it, extract the relevant information and update the case file without human involvement.

The same applies to portal updates, correspondence, invoices and all the other information that arrives during the life of a claim.

There are a few recurring examples.

**Data entry and rekeying**

The same claimant details, accident circumstances and insurer information can be entered into several different systems. A person might enter the information into the CMS, then again into the Claims Portal, MedCo or a medical agency's system.

It's not particularly complicated work, but it takes time and introduces opportunities for mistakes.

**Chasing clients, insurers and agencies**

A claim often depends on someone else doing something. A client needs to provide information, an insurer needs to respond, or a medical agency needs to arrange an appointment.

Each of these interactions needs to be followed up. When a firm has a large number of active cases, keeping track of all those outstanding actions becomes a significant administrative task.

**Reading and processing documents**

Medical records, engineering reports, hire documents and bank statements can contain hundreds of pages of information.

Someone has to find the relevant details, identify dates and figures, understand what is important and then record the findings in the case management system.

This is an area where document AI can make a particularly useful difference, provided there is appropriate human review and oversight.

**Monitoring deadlines**

Portal response times, limitation dates and court directions all need to be monitored. These dates may appear in different systems, and firms need confidence that nothing important is missed.

**Accounts and disbursements**

Invoices and disbursements also generate administrative work. Information may need to be entered into the case management system and then transferred into a separate accounts or finance system.

Individually, none of these activities looks particularly significant. But multiply them across thousands of live files and the cost starts to become rather more interesting.

This is especially important in personal injury work operating under fixed recoverable costs. The amount a firm can recover for much of the work is determined by the applicable costs regime, not simply by how many hours its employees spend on a case.

The commercial challenge is therefore quite straightforward. If the fee is constrained, the firm needs to control the cost of progressing the file.

Reducing the time spent on repetitive administrative work is one of the more obvious ways to do that.

## How can personal injury firms automate their existing software?

There are several ways to approach automation, and most firms have probably explored at least one of them.

The important distinction is between automating something inside the case management system and automating the work that happens between different systems.

**1. Extend the case management system**

The first option is to ask the CMS supplier to configure additional workflows or functionality.

This makes sense when the work takes place entirely within the case management system. The supplier understands its own software and can often extend existing functionality without introducing another platform.

However, there are limits to how far this can go. If a process starts in an external portal, an email inbox or a scanned document, the CMS may not be able to handle the entire task on its own.

**2. Build integrations in-house**

Some larger firms have internal development teams capable of building their own integrations and automation tools.

Microsoft Power Platform is a common starting point, particularly for organisations already using Microsoft 365.

For relatively straightforward processes, this can work very well. But as the number of applications, rules and exceptions increases, the complexity of maintaining those solutions grows too.

There is also a difference between building a simple automation that runs occasionally and operating a high-volume process that needs to work reliably, unattended, throughout the day.

This is where firms can find themselves spending more time maintaining their automation than they originally expected.

**3. Outsource the administrative work**

Another option is to move some of the work to an outsourced back-office provider.

This can reduce the immediate pressure on internal teams and may make commercial sense for certain activities.

However, it doesn't necessarily remove the underlying work. It simply moves it somewhere else, along with the associated cost and management requirements.

**4. Use a specialist automation platform**

The other approach is to introduce a platform specifically designed to connect the systems a firm already uses and automate the processes between them.

This is where Toca fits.

Rather than replacing the case management system, Toca works alongside it. It can read information from the firm's existing CMS, interact with external services and write the results back into the case file.

The platform currently operates alongside systems including Proclaim, Visualfiles, ActionStep, Aderant and Clio, connecting them with services such as the Claims Portal, Official Injury Claim and MedCo.

Spectral, Toca's document AI capability, can also process documents such as medical records, engineering reports and hire documents, extracting relevant information for review and approval by a lawyer.

The important point is that the firm doesn't have to start again with a new case management system. It can keep the software its teams already know and improve the way the different parts of the business work together.

## Choosing the right software for your firm

Choosing a case management system is important, but it is only part of the technology decision.

A firm can invest heavily in a sophisticated CMS and still have employees spending much of their day transferring information between portals, chasing outstanding documents and processing invoices.

Before making another software investment, I'd suggest looking at the process from beginning to end.

Start with a single claim. Follow it from the first client contact through to settlement. Write down every system it touches, every document that needs to be processed and every point where someone has to enter information that already exists elsewhere.

You may find that the biggest opportunity isn't replacing any of your existing software at all.

There are five things worth considering:

1. **Map the entire claims process.** Don't just look at what happens inside the CMS. Include portals, medical agencies, document handling, correspondence and accounts.

2. **Keep your existing CMS if it does the job.** Replacing an established system is expensive and disruptive. If the problem is the work around it, changing the CMS may simply move that problem elsewhere.

3. **Ask suppliers to demonstrate a real process.** Give each vendor the same example claim and ask them to show how it progresses without someone repeatedly entering information that is already available.

4. **Understand the cost of each administrative step.** Look at the time spent on individual activities and what that means across your total caseload. Toca's [fixed costs calculator](https://www.toca.io/legal/cost/fixed-costs) and [credit hire calculator](https://www.toca.io/legal/cost/credit-hire) can help put some figures against this using your own volumes.

5. **Make sure decisions remain accountable.** Automation can process information and prepare work, but firms still need a clear record of decisions, who made them and what evidence they relied upon. This is particularly important when using AI. See our article on [supervising AI on volume files after Mazur](https://www.toca.io/news/learn/supervising-ai-on-volume-files-after-mazur/).

Ultimately, the best technology stack isn't necessarily the one with the most features. It's the one that allows the firm to progress claims efficiently without creating unnecessary administrative work.

## Questions

### What software do UK law firms use?

Most UK law firms use a case management or practice management system such as Proclaim, Visualfiles, LEAP, Clio, Smokeball, ActionStep or Aderant. They typically use additional software for document management, client identification, anti-money-laundering checks, electronic signatures and accounts.

Personal injury firms also use specialist services such as the Claims Portal, Official Injury Claim and MedCo as part of the claims process.

### What is the best case management software for personal injury firms in the UK?

There isn't one system that is best for every firm. Proclaim, Visualfiles, LEAP, Smokeball and Clio all offer case management capabilities for personal injury work.

The right choice depends on the firm's size, case types, working practices and budget. Firms handling high volumes of claimant work should pay particular attention to portal integrations and how much manual data entry is still required.

### Does my case management system connect to the MOJ Portal?

Many case management systems connect to the Claims Portal through an application-to-application (A2A) integration. These connections allow information to be exchanged between the CMS and the portal.

Access Legal, for example, states that Proclaim supports two-way A2A integration with the RTA and EL/PL Claims Portals. However, the extent of integration varies between suppliers and systems, so it's worth asking exactly which stages are covered and what still requires manual work.

### What is the difference between the MOJ Portal and the OIC Portal?

The Claims Portal, often referred to as the MOJ Portal, handles eligible road traffic, employers' liability and public liability claims up to £25,000.

The Official Injury Claim (OIC) service handles qualifying whiplash claims on the small claims track. It is operated by the Motor Insurers' Bureau on behalf of the Ministry of Justice and was introduced on 31 May 2021.

They are separate services with different processes and requirements.

### Do we need to replace our case management system to automate our processes?

No. In many firms, the biggest opportunities for automation sit between the case management system and the other services used to progress a claim.

A specialist automation platform can work alongside an existing CMS, transferring information between systems, interacting with portals and processing documents without requiring the firm to replace its core software.
