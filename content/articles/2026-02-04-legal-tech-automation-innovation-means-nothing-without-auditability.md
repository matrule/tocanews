Innovation in the legal sector is a double-edged sword. On one side, law firms face increasing pressure to modernise, streamline operations and unlock the repository of data trapped inside their case management systems (CMS). On the other, they operate within one of the most strictly regulated, risk-averse environments in the world.

For legal tech innovators, the core challenge isn't just building faster tools: it's creating software that respects the heavy burden of compliance.

Our Sales Director, Keith Sutton, recently represented Toca at Legaltech Live in the North West to address this exact tension. Taking the floor during the Open Mic session, Keith shared how legal automation can move forward without compromising security or regulatory integrity.

![Keith Sutton speaking at Legaltech Live in the North West](2026-02-04-keith-sutton-legaltech-live.jpg)

## The compliance dilemma in legal tech

As Keith noted during his session:

> "Innovating in this field poses challenges due to regulatory requirements and the potential reputational risks associated with change. These factors necessitate that any AI and automation solutions are fully auditable, secure, and flexible to unlock the value of data within CMS systems."

For many firms, the fear of reputational damage acts as a handbrake on digital transformation. If an automated process makes an error, or if an AI-driven workflow lacks a clear paper trail, the consequences extend beyond a software bug: they touch on client confidentiality, professional indemnity and regulatory breach.

## Bridging the gap: innovation meets governance

To make legal automation truly viable, technology providers must design platforms that prioritise compliance from day one. That means focusing on three core pillars:

* **Complete auditability:** every action, data transformation and automated decision must leave a transparent, traceable trail. Firms need to know precisely *how* and *why* a workflow executed.
* **Security and control:** AI and automation tools cannot exist as black boxes. Data handling must meet rigorous security standards while safeguarding sensitive client information.
* **CMS integration and flexibility:** legacy systems shouldn't hold firms back. Automation platforms need the operational agility to interface directly with existing CMS platforms, turning static records into actionable data.
