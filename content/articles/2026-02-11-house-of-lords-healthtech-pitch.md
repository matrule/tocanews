Strategy in the HealthTech sector requires a constant balancing act between cutting-edge technology and government policy. To build tools that scale, innovators have to stay closely aligned with the institutions shaping healthcare delivery and procurement.

Toca joined the Startup Coalition at the House of Lords for their **AI in HealthTech** pitch event. Stepping into Parliament provided a valuable opportunity to hear directly from policy makers, industry leaders and frontline advocates about where NHS policy is heading and how independent technology firms can support it.

The energy in the room was palpable, but beneath the excitement of AI lay a very practical, outcome-focused conversation. Here are three major takeaways from the event.

![The Palace of Westminster on the morning of the pitch event](2026-02-11-palace-of-westminster.jpg)

## 1. Bridging the procurement gap: the Pipeline Programme

One of the central discussions centred on the launch of the **Pipeline Programme**. Historically, one of the biggest friction points for HealthTech startups has been moving from successful pilot to official government procurement. The Pipeline Programme marks a significant, deliberate step toward bridging the gap between innovative technology and public sector purchasing, creating a clearer roadmap for deployment within the NHS.

## 2. Moving beyond hype: deploying functional HealthTech

While generative AI and high-concept models dominate the headlines, the panel made one message explicitly clear: **the emphasis remains on AI solutions that deliver immediate, practical value to the frontline.** The NHS doesn't need novelty; it needs efficient, secure tools that relieve administrative burdens, streamline workflows and improve immediate patient care outcomes. Real-world execution matters far more than theoretical capability.

## 3. Agility outpaces scale

A recurring theme throughout the pitch session was speed and adaptability. Smaller, agile AI firms are increasingly proving they can outpace tech giants. By leveraging smarter data access, navigating legacy systems effectively and iterating quickly based on clinical feedback, agile platforms can solve specific operational bottlenecks in fractions of the time standard enterprise rollouts take.

## Collaborating with policy leaders

The insights shared by the panel and guest speakers highlighted the depth of thought currently driving UK health policy. It was a privilege to hear perspectives from **Baroness Dido Harding**, **Jonathan Wolf**, **Chris Bischoff**, **David Lawson MCIPS** and **Lord Jason Stockwood**.

A warm congratulations to **GutSee Health** on their well-deserved win during the pitch competition.

As we return to Toca, we're leaving with plenty to reflect on and valuable new connections across both industry and policy. Bridging the gap between intelligent automation and healthcare isn't just an engineering challenge: it's a collaborative effort across the entire ecosystem.
