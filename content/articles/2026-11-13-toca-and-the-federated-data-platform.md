Something quiet is happening in NHS Trusts. The Federated Data Platform is not mandatory, and at least 16 trusts and boards have said on the record that they don't use it. Most give the same reason: what they already have does the job.

That is not a protest. It is a judgement about where the work gets done.

## A platform that looks at the work

A data platform sits above your systems. It takes a copy of what they hold, joins it up and shows it back to you. That helps with a national picture. It helps less when there is a worklist to clear.

Its own supplier told MPs that writing back into local systems is one of the harder problems. Its headline result is 675,000 patients removed from waiting lists. Much of that is validation: finding people who no longer needed to be there. It matters. But it is work done afterwards, on a list that was wrong.

The better question is why the list was wrong.

## Where the list is made

Every entry on a waiting list began as a referral. A GP asked a question, a letter arrived, and someone decided what should happen next. If that moment is slow, the patient waits before the wait has begun. If it is recorded badly, the list is wrong from day one.

That is where Toca starts. eTriage puts the referral, the letter and the patient's record on one screen. The consultant decides. One action updates e-RS, the EPR and the document store. Nothing is re-keyed.

Eight Trusts have chosen it. At Royal Berkshire, the Trust reports a 51% reduction in referral admin workload, and 49% of Advice & Guidance requests resolved without a clinic visit.

Run the front door this way and three things change.

**The clock.** The waiting time clock starts with the referral. We handle that event, so the record of it is the event itself. Reporting becomes a log of what was done, by whom and when.

**Demand.** A waiting list shows you demand weeks after it arrived. The front door shows it the same day: which specialty, how urgent, what kind of clinic. Plan on that and you are planning on what is coming.

**The system.** When Trusts share a front door, a referral can go to where there is capacity before the patient has waited at all.

The first is live. The second and third are what we are building next, with the Trusts we already work with.

## The rest of the pathway

The same rule holds further down. We are working on discharge with Spectral, our AI engine, which reads the letters and notes a data platform never sees in usable form. We are building the longitudinal view of a patient's pathway from the actions taken along it. Do the work and the record follows.

## With it today, instead of it tomorrow

None of this asks a Trust to rip anything out. If you want your numbers in the Federated Data Platform, Toca can send them. We connect to e-RS, EPRs, PAS systems and document stores every day. One more destination is not a problem.

But notice what happens as more of the pathway runs this way. The list is right because the work was right. Demand is visible on arrival. Capacity is matched at the door. There is less and less for a platform above it to find.

The national layer still gets its numbers. It stops being where the work happens.
