**The short answer.** "UK-hosted" means one of three things, and they are not the same. A global platform running in its **UK cloud region** keeps your data in the UK but runs on the vendor's terms. A **UK vendor hosting in UK data centres** adds a UK company that answers for it. A platform you can **run in your own tenancy or on your own servers** keeps everything inside your boundary. Toca, the platform we build, is a UK company and gives you three ways to run it, chosen for each deployment: in Toca's own UK cloud, in your own cloud, or on your own servers, air-gapped if you need it. Of the others, Power Platform offers the first kind, Responsiv the second, and n8n and Responsiv's self-hosted product the third. Decide which kind your data actually needs, and then check the vendor's G-Cloud listing or contract, not just its website.

## The three kinds of UK hosting

The difference matters because each kind answers a different question from the people who look after your data, and a vendor that says "UK-hosted" may mean any one of them.

| Kind | What you get | What to check | Fits |
|---|---|---|---|
| A global cloud's UK region | Data stored and processed in the UK, on the vendor's global platform | Which features or AI services can move data outside the UK, and who can switch them on | Most commercial work where UK residency is enough |
| A UK vendor in UK data centres | UK residency, plus a UK company running the service | The data centres' certification, the sub-processors, and whether you can choose location | Public sector and regulated work that wants a UK supplier |
| Your own tenancy or servers | Nothing leaves your boundary; you control the infrastructure | Which product and plan allow self-managed deployment, and who patches it | Health, legal and financial work where data cannot leave the building |

Toca gives you three ways to run it, which between them cover the second and third kinds. It runs in Toca's own UK cloud, in your own cloud, whether that is Azure, AWS, Rackspace or a dedicated server, or on premises. You choose for each deployment, and they work together. Toca's agent bots sit inside your network and connect outbound to the Toca server, needing only internet access and an entry on your allow-list, so a process running in Toca's UK cloud can still work with a legacy case management system that is hosted on premises.

## What to ask every vendor

These seven questions sort a shortlist, and we have given Toca's answer to each.

1. **Where exactly is my data stored and processed, and can I choose?** A vendor's G-Cloud listing answers both in a standard form. With Toca it is in the UK, and you choose between Toca's UK cloud, your own tenancy and your own servers for each deployment.
2. **Can any feature move data outside the UK?** AI features are the usual exception. Microsoft, for instance, says some generative AI features can process UK data outside the UK once an administrator allows it. Spectral's models can run inside your boundary.
3. **Can it run in my own tenancy or on my own servers, and on which plan?** "Self-managed is available" often turns out to mean a particular product or tier. Toca runs in your own tenancy or on your own servers, air-gapped if the data cannot leave the building.
4. **Who are the sub-processors?** A UK data centre run under a non-UK parent may still matter to your DPIA. Toca is a UK company.
5. **What is certified?** Look for ISO 27001 for the service and Cyber Essentials Plus, for health DSPT and the clinical safety standards, and for AI ISO 42001. Toca holds ISO/IEC 27001, ISO/IEC 42001 and Cyber Essentials Plus, and Spectral is UKCA-marked as a Class I medical device.
6. **How does it reach systems with no API?** Regulated work usually involves at least one, whether an old case management system, a portal or a desktop application. Ask whether it is reached by API, RPA or code, and who maintains it. On Toca all three sit in one workflow, and agent bots inside your network connect outbound to reach the systems you still host yourself.
7. **What does it cost at your volume?** Platforms charge per user, per bot, per app or per workflow, so model three years at your real numbers. Toca is priced per workflow, not per seat.

## Questions

### What does UK-hosted mean for a low-code platform?

It means your data is stored and processed in the UK, but that can be a global cloud's UK region, a UK vendor's UK data centres, or the platform running in your own tenancy or on your own servers. Ask which one, and whether any feature can move data elsewhere. Toca is a UK company and gives you three ways to run it: in its own UK cloud, in your own cloud or on your own servers.

### Which low-code platforms can run on our own servers?

Toca can, air-gapped if the data cannot leave the building. Several others offer self-managed deployment, often on particular products or plans, including Mendix, OutSystems, n8n and Responsiv Unity. Check which features run self-managed, and not only that it is possible.

### Is Power Platform UK-hosted?

It can run in Microsoft's UK region. Systems on your own network are reached through the on-premises data gateway, and some generative AI features can process data outside the UK if an administrator allows it. If you need the platform itself inside your own boundary, Toca runs in your own tenancy or on your own servers.

### What should a DPIA check about a low-code platform?

It should check where data is stored and processed, who the sub-processors are, which features can move data, what is certified and who can access production. G-Cloud listings state several of these in a standard form, and Toca's G-Cloud 15 listings cover both the platform and Spectral.

### Are there UK low-code platforms on G-Cloud?

Yes. Toca lists both its platform and Spectral on G-Cloud 15, and Responsiv lists services there too. Search the Digital Marketplace for "low-code platform" to see the current list.

### What does a UK-hosted low-code platform cost?

It depends on the pricing model, which may be per user, per bot, per app or per workflow. Toca is priced per workflow, not per seat: typically £1 per referral in NHS acute and general hospitals, with no other costs, and £30,000 to £150,000 a year as a guide for law firms.
